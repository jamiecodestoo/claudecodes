# 8x.life: what it says, what it should say

Read top to bottom on desktop (1440×900) and on a phone (iPhone 14, 390×844), on 6 Oct 2026.
Screenshots: `shots/8x/`.
I also read 8x.careers, because "Jobs" sends every candidate there, so it's part of what they experience.
Outside context on the company is in `raw/context/findings.md`.

> **The brief:** 8x.life is our life-at-8x site. Its job is to make the right people want to work here and give the wrong people a reason to self-select out.

## 1. What is there today

| Page | What's on it | Words |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | "8x" · "Human orchestration for business outcomes · 50+ countries" · "250k+ humans managed in marketing, engineering, and research." · a list of 7 product lines (Social, LinkedIn, Research, Sales, Hiring live; Email and Global "not unlocked yet") · Manifesto / Team / Jobs | 68 |
| `/manifesto` | Six lowercase lines: ai is changing the world → anyone can build so the value lies in the distribution → jobs will change but never disappear → the human in the loop becomes the most valuable and the bottlenecked resource → 8x supplies and orchestrates those humans → implications are global and generational | 49 |
| `/team` | Jaka Bavdek, co-founder and CEO · Theo Bui, co-founder and CTO · "We're hiring." | 12 |

- **Total: 129 words across the whole site, counting navigation labels.** About 13 of them mention hiring. **None of them describe what it's like to work at 8x.**
- **The look:** Newsreader serif, black on white, one column about 576 px wide, small caps for labels. No images, no faces and no motion beyond an underline on hover. It reads like a calm, confident, well-set letterhead.
- **On the phone:** the same single column, simply narrower. Nothing breaks, and nothing is added.

## 2. What somebody actually comes away believing

I read it as three different people would, in the 30 seconds a stranger gives a page:

- **A strong candidate deciding whether to apply.** "8x is some kind of agency or holding company with a lot of sub-brands. It manages a huge number of freelancers. Minimal and confident. Looks like two people. Something about AI. I have no idea what I'd do there, or with whom."
- **A candidate who would be a bad fit.** Reaches the same conclusion, and so has *no reason to opt out*. The page never says anything a person could disagree with.
- **A client or partner.** Gets the most out of it: the product lines, the scale and the domains. **The page is written for buyers, not for future colleagues.**

**What it says today:** 8x sells human labour at scale.
**What it should say:** at 8x, you'll own something real from week one, and you'll be judged on the work.

The site is a company directory sitting where a life-at page should be.

## 3. Vague

- **"Human orchestration for business outcomes."** Category jargon. It doesn't tell you what someone at 8x does on a Monday.
- **"250k+ humans managed."** Managed how? Employees, creators, freelancers? To a candidate, "managed" and "supplies… those humans" make people sound like stock. It also leaves the most important question open: *am I applying to be one of the 250k, or to run them?*
- **"50+ countries."** 8x.careers talks about "ten markets" and "four products". Which is the real footprint for an employee?
- **The manifesto.** "ai is changing the world" and "implications are global and generational" are true of almost every company in 2026. Nothing in it is a stance a reader could push back on, and nothing turns the belief into "so here's what working here asks of you."
- **"Evidence-first hiring."** The best phrase on the site, and it's never explained.

## 5. Reads like every other company page

- The manifesto's first three lines are the consensus take on AI in 2026.
- "We're hiring." as the whole call to action.
- A product list with one-line taglines is a holding-company homepage pattern.
- On 8x.careers, "Get hired for the work you can do", "Meet the people who work at 8x" and the Engineering / Design / Marketing / Operations category row are standard careers-site patterns. The *process* is distinctive, but the presentation isn't.

## 6. Contradictions

1. **The "human company" has no humans on it.** Not one face or voice.
2. **The team page shows 2 people.** 8x.careers shows 11+, LinkedIn lists 48, and the CEO describes "a core team of six or seven people". Nobody outside can tell which of these is the team they'd join.
3. **The headline number doesn't hold up against 8x's own sources.** 8x.life says "250k+ humans managed · 50+ countries". The CEO's site says "120,000+ humans … 30+ countries", his June 2026 post says "121,000 people on the platform", and the CTO's site says "50K+ humans … 30+ countries". The same sources mention "ten markets". A company that hires on evidence leads with a number its founders don't repeat. *Recommendation: use one dated, defined figure, e.g. "121,000 people on the platform, June 2026", and say what is being counted.*
4. **The product list is out of date.** 8x Karma, a Reddit growth tool built from idea to launch in 17 days in September 2026, isn't on it. That build is the best evidence of pace 8x has, and the site leaves it out.
5. **8x asks candidates for evidence but gives none about itself.** Candidates submit real work, have it read by a person and go through a two-week trial. 8x.life offers 129 words and two names in return. This is the central problem, and also the way into the redesign.

## 6b. What the outside research adds (`raw/context/findings.md`)

- **Two kinds of "working at 8x".** There's a small core team, and a very large outcome-based, on-demand network (creators, callers, researchers). The CEO: "you don't hire the team, you buy the result." 8x.life never says which one a reader would be joining. That's the first thing the new homepage has to settle.
- **The pace is real and the founders say it openly.** "We're in the office practically 24/7." The CEO criticises Berlin because "most people leave at 6pm". This is exactly the sort of fact that makes the wrong people opt out, *if* 8x is willing to say it on its own site. Needs sign-off from 8x before it's used.
- **Hiring from work, not CVs, goes deep.** An engineer was hired "without a single interview" on the strength of hackathon wins. A design competition's "winner gets hired". The CTO: "no interview needed when you can ship fast." The CEO used assignment-first hiring at his previous company too.
- **Talent is global and credentials are optional.** "Global arbitrage is the real unlock. We have 20-year-olds from Pakistan managing six-figure contracts." Team members are visible in India, Pakistan, Turkey and Slovenia. The CEO's stated mission is "opening a path from small countries to SF."
- **Early and funded.** The company formed at Entrepreneur First London in autumn 2025 and moved to SF in January 2026. An SEC Form D in August 2026 shows $5.56M raised. Candidates deserve to know it's a one-year-old seed-stage company. That's an opt-in for some people and an opt-out for others.
- **The voice is split.** 8x.social is loud Title Case hype. 8x.sale, 8x.business and 8x Research are plain and careful. LinkedIn is lowercase and online. The manifesto borrows the LinkedIn tone. For a site judged on evidence, the plain, careful voice is the one to use.
- **A question sharp candidates will ask:** "What exactly will I be helping sell?" (creator seeding on fresh accounts, Reddit growth). The life site doesn't need to defend the products, but it should describe the work plainly, so nobody finds out after joining.

## 7. What a candidate needs before applying, and doesn't get

In the order a candidate asks:

1. **What would I own?** Concretely, in week one and by week four.
2. **How is the work judged?** Output, speed, quality? Who decides?
3. **What's the pace and the deal?** Hours, availability, remote and async norms, time zones.
4. **Who would I work with?** Faces, roles, what each person owns.
5. **What does 8x believe that others don't, and what does that ask of me?**
6. **Who thrives here, and who doesn't?** Said plainly.
7. **How does getting in work?** Assignment, trial and decision, with timings.
8. **What's the pay?** 8x asks every candidate for their *last salary* but publishes ranges only "when the role publishes one". A sharp candidate will notice that imbalance. *Flag for the 8x team: either publish ranges or explain why you ask.*
9. **What happens after the trial?** Where did past interns end up? (No evidence anywhere yet.)
10. **What's the next step?** "Jobs" is the last item, in small caps, at the bottom of the page.

## 8. The beliefs we want people to leave with

What a strong candidate should believe after 60 seconds on the new homepage. Each one comes with its evidence, because that's 8x's own standard:

| # | Belief | Evidence that already exists |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | **You'll own something real from week one.** | Interns lead a country launch; "the first dedicated designer"; "Own a full product end to end"; "4 products. 10 markets. 5 channels. one person." |
| B2 | **You're judged on the work, not your CV, and so is 8x.** | No CVs; the assignment arrives on submit; "a person reads what you send back"; an engineer hired "without a single interview" on hackathon wins |
| B3 | **Lean and fast on purpose. Hands on, no layers.** | "Small budgets on purpose"; "Hands in the account, not managing an agency"; 8x Karma went from idea to launch in 17 days |
| B4 | **The bet:** building is cheap now, so judgement and distribution are the scarce things, and 8x organises the people who supply them. **At 8x, your judgement is the product.** | The manifesto, finished |
| B5 | **It isn't for everyone, and that's deliberate.** | Two-week trial; remote; small budgets; no hand-holding |

### Who should opt in, and who should opt out

The "out" list is my inference from the listings and process. The 8x team should confirm every line before it goes live.

| Opt in if you… | Opt out if you… |
|---|---|
| would rather show your work than describe it | want your CV or pedigree to do the talking |
| want a whole problem, such as a country, a channel or a product | want a well-defined slice of someone else's problem |
| like finding what works on a small budget before anyone scales it | need a big budget or a team under you to do your best work |
| are comfortable being judged on output in a two-week trial | want a guaranteed role before you prove anything |
| work well remote and async, across time zones | need an office, a fixed routine or a manager checking in daily |
| are early in your career and want responsibility faster than a grad scheme gives it | want a structured programme and a clear ladder |
| want in early at a one-year-old, seed-stage company | want late-stage stability, published bands and a benefits package |
| are fine with the founders' pace ("in the office practically 24/7") | want hours you can predict *(8x to confirm how to phrase this)* |

## 9. The redesign thesis

> **8x hires on evidence. So 8x.life should be held to the same standard: every claim it makes about working here comes with its receipt.**

This sets the content strategy: claims plus evidence, the buried facts brought to the top, and self-selection said out loud.
It also sets the interaction strategy: the motion *shows the evidence* rather than decorating, the way e2's motion proves "friends".
It's the filter for everything that follows. If an interaction doesn't reveal, prove or sort, it doesn't go in.
